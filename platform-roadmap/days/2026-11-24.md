**Goal:** `week03/worker-pool`: N workers process a stream of jobs, and Ctrl+C stops it cleanly within a deadline, reporting what was done and what was dropped.

**First action:** Create `week03/worker-pool/main.go` with `func worker(ctx context.Context, id int, jobs <-chan int, results chan<- int)`.

**Steps:**
1. **Basic pool:** `worker` ranges over `jobs`, "works" (`time.Sleep` for a few hundred ms) and sends a result. `main` starts 4 workers with a `sync.WaitGroup` (`wg.Go`), sends 20 jobs, closes `jobs`, and prints every result. Decide who closes `results`, and when, so that `range results` ends.
2. **Stop on signal:** `signal.NotifyContext`. On Ctrl+C the producer stops sending new jobs, and workers finish the job in hand and then exit. Print `processed X, dropped Y`.
3. **Drain or abort:** make one job's work honour `ctx` (`select` on the timer vs `ctx.Done()`). Choose a policy for in-flight jobs: finish them or abandon them. Write down why.
4. **Shutdown deadline:** if the workers haven't finished within 2 s of the signal, give up and exit non-zero. It's the same pattern as the wrapper's grace period, but in-process.
5. **Test:** inject the work function. Check that every job is processed exactly once with no signal, and that cancelling the context makes the pool return (with `results` closed) within the deadline.

**Done means:**
- `go run -race ./worker-pool` prints 20 results and exits 0.
- Ctrl+C mid-run prints `processed X, dropped Y` and exits in under 2 s.
- `go test -race ./worker-pool` passes.
