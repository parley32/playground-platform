## Log

**Did:** All six steps of today's plan done (steps 4–5 of the 2026-11-17 plan). Child is started in its own process group (`SysProcAttr{Setpgid: true}`), and its group becomes the terminal's foreground group when stdin is a terminal (`Foreground: true`, `Ctty: 0`, checked with `term.IsTerminal` from `golang.org/x/term`, the first external dependency in `phase3-linux-networking/go.mod`). The child gets the wrapper's stdin/stdout/stderr directly (`cmd.Stdin/Stdout/Stderr = os.Std*`, set before `Start()`). The stdin-reading goroutine and its `select` case are gone. On SIGINT/SIGTERM, `run` sends SIGTERM to the child's group (`syscall.Kill(-pid, ...)`), treats `ESRCH` as "already exited", sends SIGCONT, then waits on `status`. `exitCode` returns 128+N for a child killed by signal N (`errors.AsType`, comma-ok `WaitStatus`, `Signaled()`/`Signal()`). Table rows added for `kill -TERM $$` → 143 and `kill -KILL $$` → 137. Checked: `echo hi | ./bin/lifecycle cat` prints `hi`. `sh -c 'exit 3' </dev/null` → 3. `kill -TERM` on the wrapper → child gone, exit 143. `kill -QUIT $$` with core dumps on → 131. `kill -9` on the wrapper orphaned `sleep`, which `systemd --user` adopted. A child that exited before `Wait()` showed as STAT `Z` for 20 s, then disappeared.

**Learned:** The terminal sends Ctrl+C (SIGINT) to its *foreground process group*, not to one process. So before `Setpgid` the child died along with the parent without any forwarding code. In its own group, the child is a background process. Reading from the terminal then sends it SIGTTIN, which stops it (STAT `T`). A stopped process acts only on SIGKILL and SIGCONT; SIGTERM is queued until it continues. That's why it's TERM, then CONT, then wait. `S` = waiting for an event, and any signal wakes it. `T` = frozen until SIGCONT. Shells give each job the foreground with `tcsetpgrp`, and `Foreground`/`Ctty` do the same thing. `/dev/null` is a character device too, so `ModeCharDevice` isn't a terminal test; `isatty` (`term.IsTerminal`) is. `kill(-pgid)` signals the whole group, including grandchildren. A wait status is a packed int (low 7 bits = signal, 0x80 = core dumped); decode it with `WaitStatus` methods, not `int(...)`. Shells and wrappers report 128+N for a child killed by signal N. SIGKILL is never delivered to the program, so no handler, `defer` or forwarding runs. Hence TERM → grace period → KILL (`docker stop`). Orphans go to the nearest ancestor that is a *child subreaper* (`PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER`, here `systemd --user`), otherwise to PID 1. A zombie is an exited process whose status hasn't been collected yet: its memory and fds are freed, but it keeps its PID and process-table entry until the parent calls `wait()`. In containers, `pids.max` is small and PID 1 inherits every orphan, which is why `tini`/`--init` exist.
