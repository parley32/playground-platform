**Learned:** A `select` on `ctx.Done()` at the top of a loop can't interrupt a read that's already blocked; something outside the goroutine has to wake it, here by setting the deadline to now. `context.AfterFunc` starts no goroutine until `ctx` is done, and `stop()` unregisters it; a hand-written `go func(){ <-ctx.Done() ... }()` per connection leaks once the connection closes normally. `ctx.Err()` is already set when an `AfterFunc` callback runs. The sender closes a channel, and `v, ok := <-ch` sees the close. A goroutine blocked in `ReadString` can't also wait on a channel, so give the read its own goroutine that feeds a channel, then `select`. A test can't detect what it doesn't observe: `TestGracefulShutdown` proves that shutdown wakes idle connections, not that `serve` waits for handlers (`wg.Wait()` ordering). Taking a `net.Listener` instead of an address makes a server testable on port `:0`. When several programs share a name, the first match in PATH runs.
