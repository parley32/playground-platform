**Learned:**
- With `NotifyContext`, the *signal* cancels `ctx`. The returned func is really `stop`: the deferred call unregisters the signal handler (so a second Ctrl+C kills the process again) and releases the context once `run` returns. By the time that defer runs, the workers have already exited (`wg.Wait()` → `close(results)` → the range loop ends), so it isn't what tells them to stop.

**Stuck / open:**
- No test covers the signal path. `TestGetReadyForWorkStopsOnCancel` cancels a ctx directly, so it proves the worker reacts to cancel, not that SIGINT/SIGTERM cancels `run`'s ctx. Belongs to step 5 (inject the ctx or the work func into `run`).
- Every job is still queued before the workers start, so there's no "stop sending new jobs" yet.
- Nit (carried over): `getReadyForWork`'s `results` param could be `chan<- int`.

**Mistakes:**
- [concurrency] Thought the deferred `cancel` from `NotifyContext` is what signals the workers. It's the signal that does that; the defer is cleanup (stop relaying signals, release ctx).
